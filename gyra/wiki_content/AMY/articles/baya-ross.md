## Overview

Baya Ross is [Albert Ross](albert-ross.md)'s wife. She is Scottish. She has been eating, continuously, compulsively, with the [Kubler](the-kubler.md) organism preventing any natural stopping point, for long enough that she is twelve metres tall. She is aware of this. She is the most reasonable person in the hotel.

This combination, enormous, potentially lethal to [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md) by accident, and entirely willing to have a conversation about it, makes her one of the game's more unusual characters. She does not want to hurt anyone. She is also, in practical terms, a structure more than a person when encountered in [Chapter 2](chapter-2.md). The game-over state she provides is that [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md) gets [eaten](eaten.md). Baya does not do this on purpose.

## Her Role in the Hotel

Baya is the hotel's matriarch in the social sense: her authority is acknowledged by most long-term residents, and her permission is required for [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md) to access [Kane's theater](the-theater-of-dreams.md), which is adults-only. She also knows where [Albert Ross](albert-ross.md) went. Whether she tells [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md) this, and under what conditions, depends on how Amy handles [Chapter 2](chapter-2.md).

She wants tea. Specifically, she wants the Flories blend that [Martha Waliger](martha-gioseppy-waliger.md) used to make for her, which Martha can no longer make because Martha is a shrub. [Martha's Tea Book](martha-gioseppy-waliger.md) still exists. The recipe is in it. This is the good solution to Chapter 2.

## The Three Solutions

The Baya puzzle in Chapter 2 has three resolutions:
1. **Good**: Find Baya's favourite blend (Flories) in Martha's annotated Tea Book. She is delighted. She tells Amy what she knows about Albert.
2. **Neutral**: Sleepy tea, easy to locate, puts Baya to sleep. Works. Less good.
3. **Bad**: The stone tea recipe from [Martha](martha-gioseppy-waliger.md)'s book, the same recipe that accidentally turned [Martha](martha-gioseppy-waliger.md) into a shrub. Applied to Baya, it turns her into a very large plant. The game considers this a [sin](sin-system.md).

## Symbolic Role

Baya is the chapter's answer to what grief looks like when it isn't directed at anyone. A woman who became monstrous not through cruelty but through absence. Albert is gone. Martha can no longer help. She's hungry, alone, and technically in charge of a hotel that no longer makes sense.
