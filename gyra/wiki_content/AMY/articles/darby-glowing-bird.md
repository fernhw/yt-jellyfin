A firebird from ancient times, Darby is a nocturnal creature that emits light to see in dark environments. While these birds are often associated with guiding weary travelers, they are more akin to sparkly cockatoos. Darby is well-trained.

[SECONDARY]
## What Darby Is
A **firebird**, a species engineered by [Dr. Elizabeth Blueth Needham](dr-elizabeth-blueth-needham.md) that emits bioluminescent light from its feathers, originally evolved (through artificial selection) to see and navigate in darkness. In game terms: [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md)'s primary light source and companion, capable of being sent to fetch items, lighting [Kubler](the-kubler.md)-infected zones, and accessing spaces Amy cannot reach.

Darby is not [Custard](custard-loevinger.md). The world of *Amy's Escape* is too grounded for that. But the symbolism is deliberate: [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md)'s lost light, her buried warmth, everything she will not let herself grieve. She attaches to Darby with a completeness that she doesn't examine.

---

## Origin
Darby was created as part of **[Dr. Blueth Needham](dr-elizabeth-blueth-needham.md)**'s second attempt to control the [Moonlight Aphid](the-12-moonlight-aphids.md) population in the hotel. The first attempt, the Moonlight Aphids themselves, had failed to suppress the [Kubler](the-kubler.md). Blueth's follow-up: engineer firebirds that would hunt the aphids.

The birds did not hunt the aphids. They wandered the hotel eating other insects and ignoring the aphids entirely. Blueth made the entire line **sterile** to prevent the experiment from spreading further. She could not bring herself to kill them outright.

She put the last remaining bird, Darby, in a storage room cage. Then she forgot about it.

---

## The Decades Alone
For a span of time measured in decades, Darby sat in that cage. [Barret Aricson](barret-aricson.md), who had been aware of the bird, also forgot. The [Kubler](the-kubler.md)'s immortality field, which prevents any biological organism within the hotel from permanently dying, kept Darby alive through what should have been starvation, then through sheer biological impossibility. It endured.

When [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md) finds it in [Chapter 1](chapter-1.md), it is in poor condition but fiercely alive. It attaches to her immediately and without reservation, with the loyalty of something that has been alone for an extremely long time.

---

## Why Darby Cannot Leave
Darby is what [Blueth](dr-elizabeth-blueth-needham.md) classifies as "biologically dead": the last of a sterile, extinct experimental line, kept alive entirely by the [Kubler](the-kubler.md)'s field. When [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md) opens the hotel exit in [Chapter 1](chapter-1.md)'s opening sequence, Darby cannot follow her through. It does not choose to come back. It comes back because the threshold that Amy can cross does not exist for Darby.

Amy does not understand this distinction at the time. Most players won't either.

## In Gameplay
- **Primary light source** in all dark areas of the [Ross Hotel](ross-hotel.md) and [Ingersole](ingersole-caverns.md)
- **Fetch mechanic**: can be commanded to retrieve small items from high or distant locations [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md) cannot reach
- **Flight**: can occupy elevated positions and light areas from above
- Darby's bioluminescence **suppresses the [Kubler](the-kubler.md)'s red-flower active stage** temporarily, making infected passages navigable
- In **[Ending E](ending-e.md)**, Amy keeps Darby when she leaves. Symbolically: she leaves with her grief buried.

---

## Notes
- Darby is the game's analogue to companions like Navi (*Zelda*), Flud (*Super Mario Sunshine*), and Lumas (*Galaxy*): a light source with personality, not an AI system.
- Darby is also a reference point for **Hewie** from *Haunting Ground* in terms of the companion-horror dynamic: a creature that is genuinely not capable of full protection but whose presence changes the emotional register of every dark room.
- The name Darby was never chosen by Blueth. Amy named it herself, in Chapter 1, without ceremony.
