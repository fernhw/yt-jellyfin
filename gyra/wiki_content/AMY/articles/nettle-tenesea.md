[SECONDARY]
## Overview

Nettle is a head with two arms. She is not technically liquid, but the distinction is functionally meaningless — she is water-stable, water-mobile, and water-dependent. She lives in any body of water she can reach by swimming through the hotel's pipe system. She is also, unfortunately for the pipes, not a small amount of water. She breaks pipes regularly just by moving through them. This is why [Barret Aricson](barret-aricson.md) has had her trapped in the public bath pool for some time.

She is comic relief. She is also, in the back half of the game, one of Amy's most useful allies.

## First Puzzle

Nettle is [Chapter 1](chapter-1.md)'s first actual puzzle. She is in the public bath tub. She is asking to be flushed — she wants out of the pool and into the wider pipe system. The complication is that Barret's room is a few metres away. If Amy attempts the flush and fails, Nettle screams. Barret wakes up. The puzzle requires getting it right, or getting it right before getting too far wrong.

This is the game's first introduction to the rule that noise has consequences.

## The Eight Flushes

After leaving the pool, Nettle appears in eight different toilets and pipes throughout the hotel, cheerfully continuing her journey toward the river. Each encounter is brief and mostly comedic — she has opinions about the cleanliness of various fixtures, specific complaints about the third-floor plumbing, and at one point appears to have gotten briefly lost. Flushing her in all eight locations triggers her appearance in the [Ingersole Miners' Hospital](ingersole-caverns.md) in [Chapter 4](chapter-4.md), where she helps [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md) escape [Barret](barret-aricson.md).

She wants to reach a river. Whether she eventually does is not confirmed in the main endings, but it is addressed in a handful of the smaller resolution vignettes.

## On the Name

Nettles are used symbolically to ward off evil. A head made of water, named for a plant that stings, whose presence marks the first clear signal to Barret that something is wrong: the name does several things at once.
