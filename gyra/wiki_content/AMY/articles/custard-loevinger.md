## Overview

Custard Loevinger was [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md)'s younger brother. He was four or five years old. He drank a cleaning product at home, and Amy was there when it happened. He died.

That is the fact. Amy does not describe it in those words. She does not describe it at all, not in any direct way. In [Chapter 1](chapter-1.md), she mentions him once — off-handedly, as though it's a small and slightly silly story — and does not come back to it. The full weight of what happened surfaces through other means: through her relationship with [Jone Ross](jone-ross.md), through the way she flinches at certain things, through what the endings reveal about why she came to the hotel in the first place.

Amy ran from home after Custard died. She did not go alone — she brought him. She found somewhere further in and hid. When she finally stopped moving, Custard was immobile. The game never shows him. Amy knows where he is. The player pieces together what that means.

## The God-Seeds and What Comes After

In certain ending paths, the existence of the [god-seeds](god-seeds.md) raises a question the game is careful not to answer cleanly: the seeds can, in principle, bring back something that has died. The implications of applying this to Custard are left as an exercise. The game's own description of what that would require — the organ harvesting that keeps [Jone Ross](jone-ross.md) going — is not presented as a comfortable thought.

## Symbolic Role

Custard's death is the engine of everything. Amy's guilt over watching it happen, her inability to process it, and her subsequent actions form the emotional architecture of the entire game. He never appears. He is always present. His structural parallel is Jone — another very young boy, frozen in time, kept in place by someone who could not let go.
