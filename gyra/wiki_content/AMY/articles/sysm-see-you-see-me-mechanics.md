*Amy's Escape* is a third-person game. SYSM is the system that makes that boundary permeable — when Amy's hands come to her face, the player sees them from the inside.

[SECONDARY]
## Overview

SYSM — See You See Me — is a studio-internal term for a specific camera and animation mechanic. The game is third-person: the player watches Amy navigate the hotel from behind and above. The perspective is fixed, except when it isn't.

When Amy covers her eyes, the camera does not cut to her face. Instead, disembodied hands come up from below the frame and cover the lens — Amy's hands, from her point of view, seen as she sees them. The third-person distance collapses. The player is inside the gesture.

The same shift occurs during close item interactions: when Amy picks something up and brings it to her face to examine it, the object enters the frame from her perspective rather than the camera's. The world doesn't change. The relationship to it does.

## Design Intent

SYSM is not used constantly. It's triggered by specific moments — moments of contact, of examination, of covering up. The effect is that a third-person game occasionally becomes something more intimate without warning. Amy's physical self, usually at a narrative distance, briefly becomes immediate. This is particularly effective in a game about a fourteen-year-old who has learned to keep her distance from everything.
