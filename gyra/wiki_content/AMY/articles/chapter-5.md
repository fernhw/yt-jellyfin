"The City Of Quitters"
Amy navigates the treacherous Ingersole Caverns and discovers the City of Quitters, home to transformed miners and their crow-person patriarch, Rulbek. As she overcomes challenges and puzzles, she gains allies for her impending confrontation with the monster in the mines, Emerette.

## Overview

Chapter 5 — *Throe* — corresponds to Acceptance in the [Kubler-Ross grief model](world-timeline.md), in its evil form. Acceptance is not peace here. It is what people look like when they have stopped fighting their circumstances and started living inside them entirely. The miners of Ingersole have done this. They are not unhappy, exactly. They are done.

## The City of Quitters

The [City of Quitters](city-of-quitters.md) is a nest in the upper levels of [Ingersole Caverns](ingersole-caverns.md), built by the miners who remained after the 1892 sealing. It is lit at all times — the lights are not aesthetic. [Emerette of the Deep](emerette-of-the-deep.md) cannot tolerate sustained artificial light. The city exists because the lights never go out, and the lights never go out because if they did, Emerette would move upward.

[Relbuk Derfla](relbuk-derfla.md) — the form [Alfred Kubler](alfred-kubler.md) has taken after two centuries in the mines — is the city's patriarch. He looks like a crow. He is lying down. He has no regrets.

Some miners transformed into white rats over decades of low-level spore exposure. Some became plant-like. All of them stayed. All of them stopped trying to leave.

## Emerette

[Emerette of the Deep](emerette-of-the-deep.md) is in the mine's lower levels. He is not a character in the narrative sense. He is a consequence. [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md)'s navigation of Chapter 5 involves understanding the city that exists because of him and what is required to deal with what remains.

## The Chapter's Emotional Architecture

Chapter 5 asks Amy to sit with people who accepted the unacceptable and found something on the other side of it. The grief stage is acceptance — but *Throe* names it as painful, not peaceful. The miners are not freed. They are still in the mine. They simply know it now. [Amy](amelia-amy-loevinger.md) has to decide what to do with that information.
