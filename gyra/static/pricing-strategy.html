GYRA — Pricing & Tier Strategy

Draft v1 · gyra.fernhw.com · Author: Alex (with GitHub Copilot as sparring partner)

The pitch in one sentence

Task-manager software is the heart of a business. All the tools in the world can't make you better if you don't know how to use them — if you repeatedly say "agile" but don't understand it. GYRA protects the process with guardrails. It prevents users and managers from undermining the process.

The financial target

Sustainability number: roughly $8–10k / month gross (one developer's livelihood + infra + Stripe fees + support time). At 400 paying customers that's an ARPU of $20–25. Every pricing decision below has to honour that number.

Self Scrum Master — the AI layer

Every paid tier includes Self Scrum Master, GYRA's built-in agentic helper. It runs through OpenRouter on our own servers (we pick the cheap, fast model for each job — GPT-mini class, Grok-fast, Haiku-class) because the work is small, structured, and bounded. We're not writing essays, we're cleaning up tickets.

We bill in GYRA AI tokens, not raw LLM tokens. A GYRA token is a unit of work, not a unit of compute. One token = one ordinary AI action (split a story, polish acceptance criteria). Heavier actions cost more tokens, in whole numbers, posted before you run them. You always see the cost before you click. Compute fluctuations are our problem, not yours.

The add-on top-up

$3.99 = 190 tokens / month. Sized on purpose to be the number a customer looks at and thinks "we can do a lot with that" — roughly a sprint or two of saved time. Available on Indie, Scholarship, Studio, and Publisher. Not available on Free — the upgrade is the path. Tokens reset every billing cycle, no rollover (release valve, not a savings account).

Scholarship abuse rule — the polite escalator. Scholarship is for builders who genuinely can't justify $9.99/mo on a board tool. If a Scholarship account is buying add-on tokens at a rate that implies ~100+ tokens/day sustained (roughly $60+/month in AI spend), that's a strong signal they can afford Indie. We email once, then the next month the account is auto-converted to standard Indie pricing ($9.99/mo) and the scholarship discount ends. No back-billing, no shame — just "you've outgrown the lane." Documented in the Scholarship terms so it's never a surprise.

BYOK — bring your own key (paid tiers only)

Indie, Studio, Publisher, and Self-host can paste an OpenRouter, OpenAI, Groq, or Anthropic key into Settings and skip the included quota entirely. Free tier cannot — AI is part of the paid promise, not a free-tier surface. Scholarship can BYOK; same abuse rule applies (if they're burning real money on a model, they can fund an Indie seat). We don't advertise BYOK loudly on the marketing page (it would cannibalize the add-on), but it's documented, supported, and never artificially throttled. Enterprise security teams love it because their data goes directly from their browser session to their vendor under their contract.

Why this token model, not "AI calls"

  • Predictable to the customer. "Split costs 1, initiative→epic costs 20" reads like a menu, not a meter. People plan against menus; they fear meters.
  • Honest about heavy work. The Speeding-Agent pass is genuinely expensive on the compute side. Charging "1 call" for it would lose money; charging "300 tokens" makes the customer aware and in control. They confirm before it runs.
  • Margin protection without metering anxiety. We run on OpenRouter, so compute cost moves under us. A whole-number token price absorbs that swing without us re-issuing pricing PDFs every quarter.
  • Latency over IQ. Cheap fast models are picked per action; the customer never sees that choice, just the token cost.
  • Drafts, always. SSM outputs are previews you approve. Token is only burned when the action runs, not when it's previewed.

What SSM will never do

  • Run on Free. AI is locked to paid tiers; Free has no add-on, no BYOK, no exceptions.
  • Auto-create, auto-delete, or auto-move stories without a human approving the diff.
  • Burn tokens on a preview, a retry due to our error, or a request the customer cancels mid-flight.
  • Train on customer data. Period. Calls are stateless; no fine-tuning, no replay, no logging of story bodies beyond a 24h debug retention.
  • Silently keep a Scholarship customer subsidised when they're spending real money on AI — the polite escalator above kicks in instead.
  • Pretend to be a real scrum master. The name is a wink. It assists; it doesn't replace the human who actually runs the room.

Switching from Jira / Linear / Notion / ClickUp / Trello? We'll match.

Teams already paying for a competitor are the highest-intent customers we can win. They know what a project board is worth, they've already opened their wallet for one, and they're switching because something hurts. The right move is to make the switch obvious, painless, and cheap.

The Switch Offer (one line on the pricing page, expandable section in the FAQ):
  • Price match on whatever you're paying your current tracker (per-seat or flat), for the equivalent GYRA tier or the next one up if our quotas need it.
  • Plus 10% off that matched price for your first year — not forever, just enough that the switch math is strictly positive in year one.
  • Free migration assistance. We pull your full project board, epics, stories, comments, attachments, and sprint history out of the old tool and into GYRA — done by us, not a script you have to run.
Proof of payment is mandatory. No exceptions. Before any match or migration begins, you upload one redacted invoice or billing-page screenshot from your current tracker, dated within the last 60 days, showing the vendor name, the plan, the per-seat or flat price, and the billing date. Bank account numbers / card digits redacted is fine — everything else stays visible.
  • No screenshot → no match, no migration, no exceptions. Not even for friends.
  • One match per organisation, ever. The discount applies to the first paid year only.
  • If we later find the screenshot was forged, the account is downgraded to the standard tier and back-billed for the difference. This is stated on the form, in plain English, before they upload.

What we migrate, and how it actually works

  • Jira: full project export via REST API — epics, stories, sub-tasks, comments, sprint history, custom fields mapped to GYRA's closest equivalent. The hairy bits (status workflow, custom issue types) we hand-map on a 30-min call before the import runs.
  • Linear: GraphQL export — cycles become sprints, projects become epics, issues become stories. Cleanest migration of the bunch.
  • Notion (databases used as trackers): CSV export per database + an interview to figure out which column meant what. The worst of the four, but doable.
  • ClickUp: JSON export — spaces become projects, lists become epics.
  • Trello: JSON export per board — lists become statuses, cards become stories, checklists become acceptance criteria.

Why this is worth doing even though it costs us hours

  • Switching cost is the #1 reason teams stay on a tool they hate. If we eat that cost, we win customers who would otherwise never have moved.
  • A migrated customer is a sticky customer. They have months of history inside GYRA on day one. Churn drops near zero.
  • It generates the best testimonials we'll ever get: “I moved 3 years of Jira tickets in one afternoon. The GYRA team did it for me.” That sentence sells the next ten Studio licenses.
  • It's bounded. One human-hour per small migration, maybe four for a Jira project with 2k tickets. At an effective $29/mo recovered, payback is 1–2 months.
Soft cap: we cap free migrations at ~2 per month in year one. If demand explodes that's a great problem — we either hire someone to run them or paywall it at $299 flat for very large projects (10k+ tickets).

The non-negotiables

  1. Full import / export on every tier. JSON, CSV, Markdown. We will never trap a user. The pricing page says, in plain text: "You can leave any day. Your data leaves with you."
  2. Headline price first, extras hidden until checkout. The pricing page shows $9.99 / $29 / $79. The per-user overage line lives in the billing UI and the FAQ, never the hero table.
  3. Per-user overage is a graceful ramp, not a cliff. +$1/user on Indie, +$2/user on Studio above 30. A 13-person team pays $10.99, not "please upgrade." Friction-free growth wins.
  4. Storage stays honest. Small included quota, transparent per-100-image expansion. The reason is published openly: "every image costs us real money to host, back up, and serve."
  5. SSO gated to Studio and above. SSO is a real support burden — fair to ask the customers who actually need it to fund it.
  6. Self-host is annual recurring, never perpetual. $12k/yr buys the binary + all updates + a quarterly office-hour call + security patches + redistribution rights inside their org.

“Is this too complex?” — honest answer

You asked the right question. Here's the trade-off laid bare:

Option A: Tiers with per-user extras (current plan)

$9.99 base + $1/user above 12 · $29 base + $2/user above 30 · $79 flat.

Pros: graceful growth, no cliff, indies don't feel punished for hiring person 13. Matches your kindness instinct.

Cons: billing logic is more code to write and test. Customer service questions will exist (“why is my bill $14 this month?”). Pricing page risks looking like Jira if extras leak above the fold.

Option B: Hard tiers, no extras

$9.99 up to 12 · $29 up to 30 · $79 unlimited. Scholarship covers anyone stuck.

Pros: brain-dead simple. Pricing page is three boxes. Billing logic is three rows. Zero support questions about overages.

Cons: a 13-person indie team feels suddenly forced into $29. That cliff could make them resent you the day they hit it.

Recommendation: ship Option B, add the per-user overage later only if you see real customer pain.

Reasons:

  • Option B is what every successful indie SaaS does at launch. Basecamp, Plausible, Linear all started with flat tiers. They added complexity only when customer demand proved the cliff was hurting conversion.
  • You can't measure cliff-pain without first seeing the cliff in action. Right now you're solving a hypothetical — “what if the 13-person team feels robbed?” Real answer: a 13-person team that values GYRA pays the $29. Most do. The ones who don't tell you, and then you add the overage line.
  • Option B is one weekend of code. Option A is a week, plus an audit trail, plus a Stripe metered-billing setup, plus a customer-service playbook. That week is better spent on the public site, the Stripe payment link, or shipping retros.
  • The scholarship covers the edge case. Any 13-person team genuinely unable to jump from $9.99 to $29 can apply. That's what the scholarship is for.
Verdict: Launch Option B (clean flat tiers). Add Option A's per-user overage in v2 only if 5+ customers complain about the cliff. Until then: complexity is a future-you problem to opt into, not a present-you cost to pre-pay.

The Free tier is for kicking the tires

Free is up to 3 users, 1 project, 6 images. It exists so a solo dev or a two-person team can spin up a board, push a few stories around, decide they like it, and upgrade to Indie when they hire person 4. No application, no friction — just sign up.

Free is not the place where a real indie team lives long-term. The caps are deliberately tight so the tier evaluates the product, not bankrolls a team. The moment a Free user adds a fourth seat or hits 6 images, the upgrade prompt is friendly, obvious, and one click.

Scholarship — the door at the bottom of the pricing page

Below the tier table, in smaller type, one line:

Indie team and need to work? Pitch us your project. We give a handful of discounts — sometimes fully free — to teams who are shipping something real and can't afford the full price yet. Apply here →
This is a one-shot deal and the page says so. The application form is blunt: “If we decline, the decision is final. You will not be able to re-apply for this project. Declining means you stay on the paid tier you would have chosen anyway. Please only apply if you've actually read this paragraph.” Kids will find the form. The wording above is what filters them — not us, not later, not a support ticket.

Why the gate has to be this hard

Every scholarship is real money out of our pocket. So the application asks for proof of intent:

  • Custom-domain email required. A @yourstudio.com address means you've already spent money on a domain, hosting, and a team identity. A @gmail.com address is an automatic no. This single rule kills 80% of low-effort applications.
  • Public socials linked. Twitter / Bluesky / Mastodon / Itch / Steam page — we need to see that the studio exists outside the application form.
  • The game itself, playable or shown. A build, a trailer, a devlog, a Steam page, an Itch listing. Something we can look at and form an opinion about. “It's a secret” is a no.
  • One paragraph: why $9.99/mo is genuinely hard right now. Honest answers welcomed. We can tell.
We may also decline projects that look too good. A team with a custom email, a polished Steam page, and a 50k follower count is clearly already spending money. They don't need the scholarship — they need the Indie tier. We say that out loud in the rejection note and offer a 20% lifetime discount instead. Most accept; the ones who don't were never going to pay anyway.

How many we actually give out

Realistically: a few full-free seats per year. Maybe 3 to 6. Each one is a real cost (storage, support time, the precedent it sets). The honest math is that one full-free Studio license costs us roughly $350/yr in infra plus whatever support hours land in our inbox.

But here's the move: almost everyone who applies and has real signal gets a lifetime discount on Indie instead. 50% off for life. $4.99/mo forever. That is cheap enough to say yes to the team, expensive enough that they're invested, and small enough that we can give it to most qualified applicants without going broke.

The split looks like:

  • ~5% of applicants get the full-free Studio scholarship (the standout cases).
  • ~60% get the 50%-for-life Indie discount.
  • ~35% get a polite decline. Most of those are kids, asset flips, or studios already too funded.
The scholarship is a marketing engine and a filter, not a charity line. “GYRA gave 6 indie studios a free pro license this year and 40 more a lifetime discount” is a story that sells more paid Studio tiers in North America and Europe than any ad ever will — and the lifetime-discount line is the one that makes the whole indie community whisper your name in the right Discord servers.

What we will not build (yet)

  • No native apps. Web-only, responsive. Native apps are a $50k/yr maintenance tax for marginal value.
  • No SSO until a Studio customer asks for it and is willing to be invoiced for it.
  • No API integrations on day one. A clean export beats a half-finished integration every time.
  • No revenue-share pricing, ever. Indies hate it, collection is impossible at this scale, and the legal scaffolding eats the margin.

The trick that protects margin on storage

Implement image optimization on upload before launching paid storage:

  • Auto-resize anything wider than 2048 px down to 2048.
  • Re-encode as WebP at quality 82.
  • Reject anything > 10 MB at the boundary (clear error: "10 MB max — please compress").

Net effect: average stored image drops from ~4 MB to ~700 KB. The 40-image Indie quota becomes effectively 100+ usable images. Customer feels generously treated. Our real cloud bill stays flat. This is a code change worth shipping before the storage add-on goes live.

Annual billing

Standard SaaS move, costs nothing to offer, improves cashflow and lowers churn:

  • Indie: $99 / yr (vs $119.88 monthly — saves 2 months)
  • Studio: $290 / yr base (vs $348 monthly)
  • Publisher: $790 / yr (vs $948 monthly)
  • Self-host: already annual.

The competitive frame

vs Trello

Trello Standard is $5/user/mo — a 20-person team pays $100/mo. GYRA Studio at the same headcount is ~$36/mo (base + 6 extra users). We're cheaper at team size, and we do the one thing Trello structurally cannot: enforce process.

vs Jira

Jira is for teams that already know Scrum cold. Most teams don't. They pick up Jira and inherit forty fields, twelve states, and zero context. GYRA is the opposite: the tool teaches the practice. That's the entire reason GYRA gets to be opinionated.

vs Linear

Linear is gorgeous and built for SaaS engineering teams. GYRA is built for studios — small, cross-discipline, where one card needs an artist + a coder + a tester to ship. Linear's one-owner model breaks that. We don't.

vs Notion / Spreadsheets

They're flexible enough to do anything, which means they do nothing well by default. GYRA's guardrails ARE the value. A workflow that's hard to break is more valuable than a blank canvas you keep redrawing.

ARPU math at 400 paying customers

Assumed mix under Option B (flat tiers, no per-user extras):

  • 60% Indie @ $9.99 → $5.99/customer
  • 25% Studio @ $29 → $7.25/customer
  • 10% Publisher @ $79 → $7.90/customer
  • 5% Self-host @ $1000/mo (amortised $12k/yr) → $50.00/customer
  • Storage add-on uptake ~15% @ $2.99 avg → $0.45/customer

Blended ARPU ≈ $73/mo per paying customer. Note this is wildly skewed by the self-host line — one self-host customer ≈ 100 Indie customers. Stripping self-host: pure SaaS ARPU ≈ $23/mo. Either way the model lands above the survival number.

Rollout sequence

  1. 1Now (pre-revenue): Add a "Pay early" Stripe Payment Link to the footer. $9.99/mo. Anyone who clicks is a real signal.
  2. 2Before Indie launch: Stripe Customer Portal. Image optimization on upload. Image-count enforcement. Export buttons surfaced clearly.
  3. 3Before Studio launch: Multi-tenant / workspace switching. Story attachments. SSO scaffolding (build only when first Studio customer offers $).
  4. 4Before Publisher launch: Audit log. Multi-workspace UX. Invoiced billing. Signed DPA template.
  5. 5Self-host: Don't list publicly. Sell only when a B2B contact reaches out. First sale validates pricing; second sale validates the channel.

The two demand-signal experiments (free, do now)

  1. Footer "Pay Early" button. Stripe Payment Link, $9.99/mo, copy: "Like GYRA? Pay early — $9.99/mo — and we grandfather you forever."
  2. The churn question. Email the next 20 users who actually complete a refinement session. One question: "If GYRA cost $29/mo for your studio, would you stay or churn?" Not "would you pay" — "would you churn". The latter separates polite yes-people from real buyers.

Open questions to settle before publishing the pricing page

  • Stripe vs Paddle? (Paddle handles VAT/MoSS globally; Stripe is cheaper but you owe the tax filings.)
  • What is the legal name on invoices — High Ground LLC, personal, or a new entity?
  • Domain plan: stay on gyra.fernhw.com until launch, or move to gyra.app / usegyra.com / similar?
  • What's the exact public copy for the "no data lock-in" promise? (1 sentence, must appear above the tier table.)
  • Scholarship: do we publish the accept/decline split openly, or keep the numbers private and only publish the recipient list? (Transparency builds trust; numbers invite haggling.)

The one-line summary to remember

Free attracts noise. Cheap attracts intent. Generous attracts respect. GYRA is cheap by default, generous by application, and never a trap.
